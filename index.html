About

Dr. Muhammad Zaman is a sociologist whose work bridges academic scholarship and public engagement. He currently serves as a Senior Research Fellow at the Maison de la Création et de l’Innovation (MaCI) at Université Grenoble Alpes, France, through the International Excellence in the Humanities Programme (2026). He also holds a tenured professorship in sociology at Quaid‑i‑Azam University (Islamabad), where he founded the Department of Sociology in 2014 and the School of Sociology in 2022.

After earning his PhD from Leipzig University in 2009, Dr. Zaman completed post‑doctoral fellowships at the University of Warsaw (Erasmus Mundus programme) and the University of Zurich (Swiss Excellence Scholarship). His research explores childhood and youth, kinship systems, social policies under scarcity, and road safety. He is developing the theoretical framework of Optimum Use of Existing Resources (OUER), a sociology of adequacy that foregrounds resource‑constrained contexts. To date he has authored three books and more than forty peer‑reviewed publications and has secured competitive research grants from the Higher Education Commission of Pakistan, the German Academic Exchange Service (DAAD) and the German Research Foundation (DFG). Beyond academia he is a regular commentator on social and policy issues in national media and contributes op‑eds to newspapers.

Recognition for Dr. Zaman’s work includes being listed among the world’s top 2 % of scientists (PLOS Biology 2020) and receiving the HEC Best Young Researcher Award in 2022. He is an approved PhD supervisor and actively mentors researchers at the doctoral and masters level.

Research Interests

Dr. Zaman’s research spans several interconnected themes within sociology. His interests include:

  • Sociology of childhood and youth
  • Family, marriage and kinship systems
  • Governance under scarcity and adequacy in the Global South
  • Road safety and mobility
  • Social policies and development

Teaching & Mentoring

Dr. Zaman is committed to high‑quality teaching and has developed and taught courses at undergraduate and postgraduate levels. His regular teaching portfolio includes:

  • General Sociology (2012 – present)
  • Research Methodology – bilingual course offered at Quaid‑i‑Azam University and virtually
  • Childhood, Youth, Marriage & Family (2012 – present)
  • Risk, Governance & Resources (2022 – present)

He supervises PhD and MPhil researchers on topics ranging from road safety and public transport to family structures and social policies in Pakistan. Several of his doctoral students have successfully defended their theses under his co‑supervision.
